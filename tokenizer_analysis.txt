1. VOCABULARY SIZE
Given that the initial alphabet (= number of unique characters in the training set +
special tokens) is 9,444, there is evidently not much room for the small BPE tokenizer
(with a vocabulary size of 10,000) to add merges that would make tokenization more
efficient -- only about 550 merges could actually be included. The Big BPE tokenizer
(with a vocabulary size of 20,000) has way more room for merges, which in theory should
make it much more efficient.

---------------------vocab_size---------------------
            |     en     |     tr     |     zh     |
Char-level  |        9444|        9444|        9444|
Small BPE   |       10000|       10000|       10000|
Big BPE     |       20000|       20000|       20000|


2. AVERAGE UNICODE CHARACTERS PER TOKEN
As expected, the small BPE tokenizer is only marginally more efficient than the character-
level tokenizer, the difference being the 550-ish merges it was able to cram into its
vocabulary. The big BPE tokenizer is much more efficient.

---------------avg_unicode_per_token----------------
            |     en     |     tr     |     zh     |
Char-level  |     1.00085|     1.00085|     1.00085|
Small BPE   |      1.1036|      1.1036|      1.1036|
Big BPE     |     2.52545|     2.52545|     2.52545|


3. TOTAL TOKENS
As expected, the character-level tokenizer results in the most tokens and the big BPE model
trailing in the fewest. For English and Turkish the small BPE tokenizer needs about half
the number of of tokens as the character-level tokenizer, and the big BPE tokenizer only a
third. For Chinese, however, the improvement is only marginal for the small BPE tokenizer and
less than 50% for the big BPE tokenizer. This is to be expected, however, since there are
so many Chinese characters and since each character contains so much meaning.

--------------------total_tokens--------------------
            |     en     |     tr     |     zh     |
Char-level  |      368269|      368383|      368266|
Small BPE   |      174617|      183138|      346050|
Big BPE     |      107828|      106077|      267425|


4. AVERAGE TOKENS PER SENTENCE
Here, the few BPE merges for Chinese means that the average token per sentence is actually
somwhat comparable for all three tokenizers. BPE clearly benefits English and Turkish more.
As expected, however, average tokens per sentence goes down for all languages when going
from character-level to small BPE to big BPE, since especially the big BPE tokenizer has
many bigger tokens to play with.

----------------avg_tokens_per_sent-----------------
            |     en     |     tr     |     zh     |
Char-level  |     81.1165|       98.63|     38.5457|
Small BPE   |     38.4619|     49.0329|     36.2204|
Big BPE     |     23.7507|     28.4008|     27.9909|


5. PATTERNS FROM SAMPLE SENTENCES
Below are the different tokenizations for nine sample sentences: three for each language. The
BPE tokenizers use ▁ instead of whitespace due to the Metaspace pre-tokenizer in the
transformers library.

In English, the small BPE mostly picks up on subwords ("un", "ed") and short, common
words ("was", "to"). The big BPE instead picks up on most regular English words, with
uncommon words, names, and punctuation being split up ("cor als," "Az ol ine", "Henry 's").
The big BPE tokenization is actually quite readable.

In Turkish, the difference between small and big BPE is surprisingly small (to someone 
who doesn't know Turkish). Both seem to pick up on (what I assume to be) common suffixes 
("an", "ine"), and the big BPE tokenization is only marginally more readable than the small
one.

In Chinese, the small BPE tokenizer performs basically the same as the character-level
tokenizer, with the only real difference being Western words and numbers that came "for
free" due to English and Turkish ("ord", "Imp", 50). The big BPE tokenizer actually merges
some characters ("來的", "辦公室"), but only very rarely more than two.

I guess I am a bit surprised that not more punctuation marks were included in merges. I also
find it interesting that some seemingly arbitrary number combinations "make it" (19, 50) in
the small BPE tokenization, whereas others do not (4 6, 7 0). Investigating the order of 
number merges when increasing BPE vocabulary size would be an interesting study. Something
else that could be interesting to study would be how much "help" e.g. Turkish gets from
English merges, and vice versa.
